## 1. Product thesis

Serbizyu is a provincial services-and-goods marketplace foundation for people who are often excluded from smartphone-first commerce.

It helps local Buyers/Customers find Providers/Owners, agree on a useful piece of work or product, complete it through a clear fulfillment shape, and preserve enough evidence for trust and support.

It is designed for the real conditions of Tagudin and similar communities:

- Cash remains important.
- Many providers are informal or assisted.
- Some users have feature phones or low digital confidence.
- Trust depends on identity, consent, evidence, reputation, and human support—not only on a payment button.
- Small work, including ₱50–₱100 errands and side hustles, can be economically and socially meaningful.
- A local marketplace must support both digital-native users and people reached through Agents or assisted access.

Serbizyu is not merely an online catalog, a gateway checkout, an escrow wrapper, or an AI chatbot. Its durable value is the shared contract connecting listing, order, work, evidence, trust, payment lane, and support.

## 2. Working-backwards press release

### Serbizyu begins a controlled Tagudin validation

**TAGUDIN, ILOCOS SUR — Future pilot announcement** — Serbizyu is building a community-centered marketplace where people in Tagudin can discover local services and goods, request help, offer skills, and complete practical work with clearer expectations and evidence.

The product is designed for the provincial economy rather than assuming that everyone has a credit card, constant connectivity, or the confidence to navigate a complex marketplace alone.

A Provider/Owner may publish a service or product listing. A Buyer/Customer may discover it, request a service or product, or receive an approved response. The work then follows an appropriate shape—such as a linear project, appointment, handoff, or digital delivery—rather than forcing every transaction into one generic order screen.

The initial Tagudin validation will aim to onboard approximately 30 genuine community participants, with a working composition of Providers/Owners and Buyers/Customers, and will measure whether real people complete valuable transactions safely and repeatedly. The pilot will begin with External Cash and External Digital Proof, where Serbizyu records evidence but does not claim custody of money.

The capstone will also demonstrate Direct Digital and Tiwala Protected Digital behavior in sandbox conditions so the technical and product boundaries can be tested without claiming live-money readiness.

The longer-term startup foundation is broader than the first cohort. It may later support additional categories, work shapes, access channels, and legally cleared connected-payment modes, but each expansion will require evidence, operational ownership, safety review, and an explicit activation decision.

## 3. The problem

### For Providers and Owners

Local workers, sellers, and small providers often depend on word-of-mouth. They may have real skills but lack:

- A structured digital listing
- Clear availability and capacity
- Evidence of completed work
- A way to receive requests beyond personal contacts
- Safe assistance from someone who can manage the platform
- A fair way to communicate terms before work begins

### For Buyers and Customers

People looking for local help often face:

- Unclear pricing or scope
- Difficulty finding available providers
- Weak identity and trust signals
- No shared record of what was agreed
- Anxiety about paying before work is complete
- No clear evidence path when something goes wrong
- Interfaces that assume high literacy, constant connectivity, or digital-payment familiarity

### For the community

A digital marketplace can unintentionally exclude the people it claims to serve if it requires:

- Smartphone ownership
- Digital payment access
- Large minimum transaction values
- Complicated forms
- Unsupported guarantees
- Silent Agent authority
- Unclear physical-safety expectations

## 4. Product promise

For a supported Tagudin capability, Serbizyu will provide:

1. A clear listing and request model.
2. A fulfillment shape appropriate to the work.
3. A visible amount, scope, capacity, or quote status.
4. A payment/trust lane whose custody and protection promise is explicit.
5. Evidence of important actions and confirmations.
6. Consent and attribution for assisted actions.
7. Safety and reporting guidance appropriate to the category.
8. A support and dispute path proportionate to the lane.
9. A low-literacy and low-bandwidth path where the pilot matrix marks it as supported.
10. A record that can be corrected through new events without silently rewriting history.

## 5. What Serbizyu does not promise

The initial product does not promise:

- That every category can be safely enabled at launch.
- That every payment is held or protected by Serbizyu.
- That an uploaded payment screenshot proves cleared funds.
- That External Cash can be automatically refunded or recovered.
- That Direct Digital is equivalent to Tiwala Protected Digital.
- That Tiwala Protected Digital is legal escrow or insurance without clearance.
- That an Agent owns the listing, money, goods, or fulfillment.
- That a Kiosk holds cash or operates a float.
- That offline devices can authorize digital payment or release.
- That a sandbox gateway proves a production contract.
- That Tagudin validation proves province-wide scale.
- That the first pilot must generate platform revenue.
- That an AI assistant can make financial, dispute, consent, or publication decisions for users.

## 5.1 Payment vocabulary in the product vision

The product uses four explicit payment lanes:

- External Cash
- External Digital Proof
- Direct Digital
- Tiwala Protected Digital

Each Order may contain one or more **Payment Obligations** for defined purposes such as a deposit, milestone, final balance, approved reimbursement, purchase-on-behalf budget, or service/protection fee. One Payment Obligation uses one lane initially; payment confirmation remains separate from Work completion.

## 6. Initial Tagudin validation goal

The first real-user cohort is approximately 30 genuine onboarded participants.

Working target:

- Approximately 10 Providers/Owners
- Approximately 20 Buyers/Customers
- At least 5 participants experiencing an assisted or low-literacy-compatible path
- Approximately 4 live category families if supply and safety permit
- Approximately 30–40 completed genuine orders as a target, not a guarantee

The initial pilot will measure:

- Activation
- Time to first useful interaction
- Provider supply and Buyer demand
- Response/liquidity
- Completion
- Repeat behavior
- Payment evidence
- Disputes and safety
- Agent consent
- Support burden
- Operating cost and expansion learning

A smaller cohort can produce useful qualitative learning but cannot be reported as conclusive market validation unless the scorecard evidence floor is reached.

## 7. Product planes

### Plane A — Capstone software

The capstone must demonstrate a coherent platform with:

- Four listing types
- A1, A3, A4, and A9 work shapes
- Direct Booking and at least one request/quote path
- Agent consent and scoped assistance
- External Cash and External Digital Proof
- Direct Digital sandbox behavior
- Tiwala sandbox behavior
- Evidence, support, dispute, and recovery flows
- Low-literacy and low-bandwidth behavior for committed access tiers

Demonstration data, team users, and sandbox financial events remain capstone evidence only.

### Plane B — Tagudin validation

The initial real-user pilot prioritizes:

- Service Listings
- Product Listings
- Direct Booking
- Agent-Mediated support
- A1/A3/A4/A9 where each complete contract is ready
- External Cash
- External Digital Proof
- Tested access tiers only
- Tagudin-only discovery and operations

Requests, Quick Deal, kiosk assistance, additional categories, and other mechanisms are conditional until their activation gates pass.

### Plane C — Startup foundation

The product is designed to expand later through:

- Additional category families
- Additional fulfillment adapters
- Additional transaction mechanisms
- Additional access channels
- Provider and community growth
- Legally cleared connected payments
- Better evidence/reconciliation automation
- Reusable onboarding and support playbooks

Expansion is earned through evidence and activation records, not assumed by the original vision.

## 8. Core product loop

### Provider/Owner loop

1. Register or receive approved Agent assistance.
2. Complete the applicable identity/consent process.
3. Create a listing or approve an Agent-created listing.
4. Define price/quote mode, availability/capacity, area, safety notes, and fulfillment shape.
5. Receive a booking, request, or qualified quote interaction.
6. Confirm scope and payment/trust lane.
7. Perform the Work Instance.
8. Submit required progress/completion evidence.
9. Receive Buyer/Customer confirmation or respond to a dispute.
10. Build a trustworthy history without fabricated review or verification claims.

### Buyer/Customer loop

1. Discover a listing or create a request.
2. Compare scope, amount, availability, trust indicators, and safety information.
3. Select an available payment/trust lane.
4. Confirm the obligation and understand what Serbizyu does and does not protect.
5. Track work, appointment, handoff, or digital delivery.
6. Confirm completion, report a mismatch, or file a supported dispute.
7. Receive a transaction record and optionally review the completed interaction.
8. Return when the service creates enough value to justify another transaction.

### Assisted loop

1. Owner grants Agent permission.
2. Agent performs only permitted actions.
3. Owner receives the required notice or approval request.
4. The system attributes the action to the Agent and affected Owner.
5. Owner may revoke or suspend the relationship.
6. Agent does not gain automatic money, goods, identity, or custody authority.

## 9. Trust model

Trust is layered rather than represented by one badge:

- Phone/account verification
- Government-ID verification with privacy and manual-review controls
- Optional category-appropriate verification
- Owner consent for Agents
- Clear payment-lane custody language
- Evidence and immutable event history
- Completion and review eligibility
- Safety guidance and reporting
- Human support and dispute records
- Cautious treatment of new Providers

Serbizyu must never let a badge, screenshot, AI suggestion, or Agent action imply more certainty than the underlying evidence supports.

## 10. Low-barrier design principles

- Cash remains valid at practical order amounts.
- Small jobs are not rejected merely because they are small.
- External Digital Proof offers a lower-barrier digital evidence path without pretending Serbizyu controlled the payment.
- Fees are shown in concrete amounts before connected-payment confirmation.
- Cash is recommended when gateway overhead is disproportionate.
- One clear primary action is preferred for low-literacy users.
- Plain Filipino/English wording and future Ilocano support are treated as product requirements, not decoration.
- Icons supplement words rather than replacing them.
- Serbi may assist comprehension and draft data, but the user confirms important actions.
- Offline operation fails safely and never authorizes digital payment or payout.

## 11. Safety and responsibility boundary

In-person and category-sensitive safety is part of product design:

- Public or safer meeting guidance where practical
- No home-entry requirement by default
- Minimal exposure of personal contact/location data
- Block, report, and incident escalation
- Category-specific restrictions and warnings
- No unsupported emergency or legal guarantee
- Clear responsibility when parties exchange cash or goods outside Serbizyu custody

The product will not create a generic “trusted” label that overrides a category-specific safety risk.

## 12. Startup learning questions

The initial cohort must help answer:

1. Which local category families generate genuine repeat value?
2. Can Providers/Owners complete the listing and fulfillment contract with limited assistance?
3. Does Agent assistance increase access without creating unauthorized action or dependency?
4. Can Buyers understand the difference between Cash, External Digital Proof, Direct Digital, and Tiwala?
5. Which requests receive enough supply to justify a real request mechanism?
6. Which work shapes fit local needs without expensive custom operations?
7. What support burden does each category create?
8. Can the platform create a durable trust/evidence history without holding every payment?
9. Which later monetization path could be transparent, collectible, and legally supportable?
10. Which expansion capability should be tested next, and what evidence would justify it?

## 13. Phase 1 exclusions and future options

### Not an initial promise

- Province-wide coverage
- All 28/29 category variants at equal readiness
- Production Direct Digital
- Production Tiwala
- Air-gapped digital payment
- Unbounded Deal-Chaining
- Kiosk cash deposits
- Agent cash/goods custody
- Full transport/dispatch infrastructure
- Full emergency-response service
- Recurring/open-ended billing
- High-risk regulated categories without a dedicated gate
- Automatic AI decisions for disputes, money, identity, or publication

### Preserved future options

- Additional work-shape adapters
- Better product inventory/capacity handling
- Quote and reverse-bidding expansion
- Safe Quick Deal expansion
- Dispatch and transport capabilities
- Rental and recurring work
- Community channels and connectors
- Serbi assistance improvements
- Provider-owned integrations
- Production connected payments after G6
- Geographic expansion after evidence and operating capacity
